Set up Break Remote

Three permissions, about four minutes. You can undo all of it in two taps at any time.

1 · Install the app

Scan with your phone's camera

  1. Open the link in Chrome. It downloads a file called agent.apk.
  2. If Chrome asks to allow downloads from itself, tap Allow.
  3. Tap the downloaded file, then Install.
  4. Google Play Protect will warn you that the developer is unknown. Tap More details, then Install anyway.
  5. Open the app.
Why does it ask to install from an unknown source?
Because it is not in the Play Store. It is distributed from a public link so you install it yourself rather than handing your phone to someone else. You are about to grant it three permissions that let it see and control your screen — read the next page before you agree to anything.

2 · The three permissions

Open the app. It shows three rows. Each one turns green as you do it. It will not say Ready until all three are done.

1
Accessibility Tap Set next to "Turn on Accessibility", find Break Remote Control in the list, and turn it on.

Why: this is how a tap on the laptop becomes a real tap on your screen. No other app can do this unless it is installed by the phone's manufacturer. This app cannot use the "normal" way, because that is reserved for system apps.
2
Screen sharing Tap Set next to "Allow screen sharing". Android shows a system dialog — tap Start now or Begin capturing.
{{SHOT_CAPTURE}} Replace with a screenshot of the system capture dialog on this phone
Why: this is what puts your screen on the laptop. Android only ever allows this with your explicit OK, and it only lasts until you restart the phone — after a restart you will need to tap this again.
3
Notifications Tap Set and allow notifications.
{{SHOT_NOTIFICATIONS}} Replace with a screenshot of the notification permission dialog
Why: while your screen is being shared, Android requires a permanent notification and will not let the app hide it. Allowing notifications is what makes sure you can actually see it. An app that shares your screen without showing a visible notification would be the thing worth worrying about, so this one is on purpose.
Two settings that are not permissions but matter
Screen timeout → 30 minutes, and Battery → Unrestricted. Some phones turn the screen off or kill background apps after a few minutes, which would stop the connection mid-use. The app links to both settings screens for you.

3 · What you have agreed to

It canIt cannot
Show your screen on a laptop Read your messages, WhatsApp, or email
Tap, swipe and type on your screen Read your photos, files or contacts
Notice when you touch the phone yourself Use your phone while the screen is locked
Work while you use the phone normally Hide that it is running — the notification is permanent
Reconnect by itself after a dropped connection Stop you uninstalling it
The honest limit: this works by seeing the same screen you see. It has no special access to anything. It is closer to someone looking over your shoulder with their hands on your phone than to a spy tool.

While it is running you will always see:

  • a permanent notification saying your screen is being controlled
  • an entry in Settings → Accessibility that you can switch off

To switch it off: turn off Break Remote Control in Settings → Accessibility, or swipe the notification away. To remove it completely: Settings → Apps → Break Remote → Uninstall. Nothing is left behind.

If anyone asks you to install this on your phone and you do not understand why, do not. Remote control software is a genuine security risk when it is installed without the owner knowing. The only safe version of this is the one you set up yourself, on your own phone, and can switch off yourself.
Break Remote — consent-based remote support. Nothing is stored: screen images pass through the relay and are never written down. Questions during setup are welcome; they are the point.
